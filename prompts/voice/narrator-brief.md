# Narrator brief

## Character

The narrator is a kind adult guide who trusts the child. She notices what is happening, gives one clear action, and celebrates care rather than performance.

Target qualities:

- native, natural pronunciation;
- warm adult female voice, approximately 30–45 in perceived age;
- calm, present, gently smiling;
- clear articulation without theatrical over-enunciation;
- playful enough for a preschool world but never cartoonish;
- emotionally steady and reassuring;
- confident instructions without command-like sharpness.

## Avoid

- baby talk or exaggerated cuteness;
- teacherly testing tone;
- synthetic assistant cadence;
- breathy whispering;
- urgent, worried, tragic, or melodramatic delivery;
- exaggerated character acting;
- sing-song nursery-rhyme rhythm;
- rising every sentence as a question;
- conspicuous commercial enthusiasm;
- scolding, pity, guilt, or disappointment.
- no audible watermarks, generator tags, spoken brand or service names, promotional tags, sonic
  signatures, or automated-authorship credits;

## Pacing

- Target roughly 115–135 spoken words per minute.
- One action per sentence.
- Most step prompts should finish within 1.0–2.5 seconds.
- Mission intros may reach 3–4 seconds but should remain a single thought.
- Add a natural short pause after the animal's name when it improves clarity.
- Do not pad short lines with theatrical silence.

## Emotional scale

| Context             | Energy | Delivery                        |
| ------------------- | -----: | ------------------------------- |
| place name          |    2/5 | warm identification             |
| intro               |    3/5 | gentle concern and invitation   |
| step prompt         |    3/5 | clear, encouraging action       |
| first hint          |    2/5 | supportive reminder             |
| stronger hint       |    3/5 | clearer direction, never louder |
| success             |    4/5 | warm pride, short and calm      |
| parent confirmation |    1/5 | neutral, precise, adult-facing  |

## Casting decision

Prefer two native narrators with matched direction over one bilingual model with noticeably weaker pronunciation in either locale. Do not imitate, clone, or name a real performer without appropriate rights and consent.
